# Evidence: module overlap with competitor cybersecurity programmes (Claim C, Claim B, incrementality)

Status: v1, 17 Oct 2026 (Step 41). Tables: `reports/module_gaps.md`, generated by
`python -m study_program_intelligence.analysis.module_gaps`; module table from Step 40 (`data/interim/modules.csv`).
Method decisions: docs/decision_log.md (2026-10-17, Step 41).

## Question

How much of what a typical competitor cybersecurity programme teaches does EMI already teach, and which topics are
missing? This tells us (a) what a new OTH cyber programme would have to add (Claim C, fit and effort), (b) how it
would differ from competitors (Claim B), and (c) how much it would overlap with existing EMI programmes (incrementality).

## Method in short

- Competitors: the 7 dedicated cyber programmes within 150 km with a module handbook (bachelors: FAU, TH Ingolstadt,
  TH Deggendorf, THWS; masters: TH Ingolstadt Cloud Applications und Security Engineering, TH Deggendorf, HDBW).
  HS Aalen and TH Mannheim only have module names: reported separately, outside the headline.
- EMI: Industrie-4.0-Informatik, Medieninformatik, Elektro- und Informationstechnik (first study section only),
  KI (B.Sc.); for masters also KI (M.Sc.), IT und Automation, AI for Industrial Applications.
- Each competitor module is compared with every EMI module (bachelor vs EMI bachelors; master vs EMI bachelors + masters).
  It counts as "covered" if its most similar EMI module is above a threshold.
- Similarity: multilingual-e5-base sentence embeddings (headline) and character-n-gram TF-IDF (independent cross-check).
  Thresholds calibrated on a topic reference set at the best-match level.
- Modules are split into security-specific, general foundations (maths, programming, systems, soft skills) and frame
  (thesis, internship, elective slots; left out). Headline = ECTS-weighted share of a programme's security ECTS
  covered by EMI; "typical" = median over the programmes.
- Security modules are grouped by CyBOK 1.1 knowledge area (plus "Penetration testing & auditing", "AI & security"
  and "Broad / introductory security" as transparent extra groups) and flagged OT/industrial by keywords.

## Validation

- Hand check by Soma of 20 competitor/EMI pairs (10 near the threshold, 5 clearly covered, 5 clearly gap):
  e5 correct in **16 of 20**, TF-IDF correct in **14 of 20**. Decision (option a): e5 is the headline, TF-IDF is
  reported as a sensitivity range.
- **All 4 e5 errors are the same kind: an advanced competitor module matched to an EMI basics module** (Threat
  Intelligence -> Informationssicherheit; Sichere Netzwerkarchitekturen -> Informationssicherheit; Advanced Database
  Techniques -> Datenbanksysteme; Software-Design, Software-Architektur und Datenbanken -> Datenbanksysteme). e5
  therefore tends to **overestimate** coverage: the headline numbers are **upper-end estimates**.
- The two methods agree on 74 % of covered/gap labels (target was 80 %; not met, see decision log). Where they
  differ, TF-IDF is more generous (it also matches shared vocabulary such as "Sicherheit", "Netzwerk").

## Headline: share of a typical competitor programme's ECTS that EMI already covers

| Competitor programme | Security modules: e5 (headline) | Range over programmes | TF-IDF (sensitivity) | General foundations: e5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cyber bachelor vs EMI bachelors | **31 %** | 18-39 % | 47 % | 71 % |
| Cyber master vs EMI bachelors + masters | **39 %** | 0-67 % | 45 % | 46 % |

Reading: EMI already teaches most of the general foundations of a cyber bachelor (maths, programming, databases,
networks, theory), but **at most about a third of its security content**, and less if the e5 overestimate is
taken into account. A cyber bachelor at OTH would need roughly two thirds of its security modules to be new.

Per programme (e5, security ECTS covered): FAU 39 % (of 140 ECTS incl. its large elective catalogue), TH Ingolstadt
18 %, TH Deggendorf 39 %, THWS 24 %; masters: TH Ingolstadt CASE 67 % (only 15 security ECTS), TH Deggendorf 0 %
(TF-IDF 100 %: the largest disagreement; its modules are industrial/automotive security, which TF-IDF matches on
shared vocabulary with IT und Automation), HDBW 39 %.

EMI's own security modules (6): Grundlagen der Codierungstheorie und Kryptologie and Informationssicherheit
(Industrie-4.0-Informatik / Medieninformatik), Cybersicherheit, KI Sicherheit and Moderne Anwendungen der
Kryptographie (KI M.Sc.), Grundlagen der Informationssicherheit und Funktionalen Sicherheit (IT und Automation).
They cover cryptography and introductory/network security; nothing in the practical/defensive core.

**The practical and defensive core that 5-6 of 7 competitors teach is missing at EMI:** penetration testing and
auditing, forensics, security operations/incident response, plus software security, security management, law and
human factors. This is what a new programme would have to build (staff and labs: Claim C feasibility).

## OT/industrial security

- Competitors with OT/industrial security content: **TH Deggendorf** (105 km) in the bachelor (3 modules, 15 ECTS,
  e.g. IEC 62443 in "Management von IT-Sicherheit") and across its master (8 modules, 50 ECTS, incl. "Secure Product
  Development for Industrial and Automotive Applications", "Industrial and Automotive Communication and Network
  Security"); HDBW (1 module, IoT/industrial internet); HS Aalen ("Industrial Security / OT Security", name only);
  TH Mannheim (Security + Industrial IoT focus, names only).
- EMI: no OT/industrial security module; IT und Automation and Industrie-4.0-Informatik have the automation side.
- Consequence: an "industrial/OT security" profile is still a differentiator against FAU, THI, THWS and HDBW, **but not
  against TH Deggendorf** (105 km, bachelor and master), which already teaches it. (Correction 18 Oct, Step 55 review:
  TH Ingolstadt, 82 km, is the nearest competitor with a cyber master.)

## Incrementality: overlap with OTH's own Informationssicherheit focus

Industrie-4.0-Informatik and Medieninformatik alone cover 28 % of a typical competitor bachelor's security ECTS (e5;
EMI as a whole: 31 %) and 13 % of a master's. So most of the security content of a new cyber bachelor would be new to
OTH, but the introductory security and cryptography part overlaps with these two programmes. The cannibalisation
check (kill rule 1, docs/overlap_test.md) should treat that part as shared modules, not as new teaching load.

## Names-only programmes (indicative)

HS Aalen (Cyber Security M.Sc.): 3 of 11 security modules covered by EMI on title similarity (IT-Sicherheitsmanagement,
KI-gestützte IT-Sicherheit, IT-Sicherheit in Dienstleistungsunternehmen); forensics, incident response, cyber defence,
penetration testing and OT security are not. TH Mannheim: Secure Coding covered; Angewandte Kryptographie and
Projektlabor Security not. Same pattern as the handbook programmes.

## What this means for the argument

- Claim C (fit): EMI can carry the foundations (about 70 % of a cyber bachelor's general modules) and cryptography /
  introductory security, but has to add the practical security core: about two thirds of security ECTS. In the four
  competitor bachelors the security modules EMI does not cover add up to 40-85 ECTS on offer (median about 60, incl.
  electives). This is a feasibility cost, not a kill reason, but it must be priced in (staff, labs).
- Claim B (differentiation): an OT/industrial profile differentiates against most competitors, not against TH Deggendorf.
- Incrementality: limited overlap with existing EMI programmes (introductory security, cryptography).

## Caveats

- e5 overestimates coverage (4 of 4 errors in the hand check were false "covered"): treat 31 % / 39 % as upper-end.
- "Covered" is binary at module level: a basics module counts as covering an advanced one (see validation). Partial
  matches (e.g. "Betriebssysteme und Netzwerke" vs "Computernetzwerke": networks only) count as covered.
- The two methods agree on only 74 % of labels; the TF-IDF range (47 % / 45 %) is the generous bound.
- Competitor ECTS include elective catalogues (FAU: 140 security ECTS), so per-programme shares are "share of what
  is offered", not "share of what every student takes". Missing ECTS were set to 5 (6 modules).
- EMI side incomplete: the E&IT handbook covers only its first study section; SPO 222B still missing.
- Module handbook texts describe intentions, not actual teaching; 2 THI masters without a published handbook are missing.
