## Evidence asymmetry and how it was handled

**Before ranking, the Step 47/48 capture and cannibalisation model was run unchanged for all three options so that
none is scored on a model the others lack; where an option had no public intake anchor (robotics) or only one
(master), this is stated and its range is widened, and weak evidence widens a score's range without raising its
centre (rule added to docs/kill_rules.md).**

## Reading for the argument

- **The Cybersecurity B.Sc. ranks first in 92 % of draws (93 % if robotics cannibalises 50 %), above the 80 %
  robustness bar,** and it stays first whichever single criterion is left out.
- **Less robust when demand is measured on German entrants:** with German-only trend scores for all three options the
  bachelor is still first, but in 78.2 % of draws, just below the 80 % bar; the master rises to 19.5 %.
- **The lead rests mostly on student demand and labour relevance.** Without demand its first-place probability falls
  to 67 % (robotics 31 %, helped by regional contribution and local jobs); without labour to 78 % (the master gains).
  Demand is also where the cyber evidence is best (rising Informatik entrants and graduates, two intake anchors) and
  the robotics evidence worst (falling feeders, no anchor).
- **Second place is the master** (67 % rank 2; 83 % with realistic robotics cannibalisation). It is held back by
  differentiation (6 cyber masters within 150 km, OT already at Deggendorf) and a low regional effect. Robotics is
  last in 72 % of draws.

## Caveats

- With the earlier skewed robotics range 2 / 3 / 5 the cyber B.Sc. still wins 87.8 %; the winner does not depend
  on this choice.
- Cyber B.Sc. labour meets all three anchor conditions (would be 5); it was scored 4 (3-5) because keyword ad counts
  overstate specialist demand. Scoring it 5 would only widen its lead.
- Anchors for criteria other than demand were written after the evidence was collected (before the ranking).
- Capture ranges rest on capacities and one anecdote, not measured intakes; real intakes of THI/Deggendorf
  programmes would narrow every demand range.
- Scores are independent across criteria within an option (except the shared capture factor); in reality fit and
  feasibility are correlated.
