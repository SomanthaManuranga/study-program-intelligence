# Evidence: demand forecast for Informatik and the cyber proxy (Claim A)

Status: v1, 17 Oct 2026 (Step 46). Tables: `reports/forecast.md`, data `data/interim/forecast.csv`, generated by
`python -m study_program_intelligence.analysis.forecast`. Method decisions: docs/decision_log.md (Step 46).

## Data and series

- First-year students (1st university semester, winter semester), Destatis GENESIS 21311-0012 (Germany) and
  21311-0015 (Bavaria), 2005-2025.
- **Cyber proxy** = Informatik + Ingenieurinformatik/Technische Informatik + Computer- und Kommunikationstechniken.
  Destatis has no cybersecurity subject; cyber programmes are classified under Informatik. The proxy measures the
  pool cyber programmes recruit from, not cyber demand itself (Trends, Step 37: cyber interest has plateaued since 2022).
- 2025 is the Bavarian G9 transition year (Bavaria all subjects 68,872 -> 57,216; it also lowers the German total),
  so all models are trained to 2024.

## Backtests (rolling origin from 2012, horizons 1-5 years, MASE)

| Series | Drift | ETS (damped) | Naive | Share x actual total* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DE Informatik | **1.02** | 1.12 | 2.49 | 3.83 |
| DE cyber proxy | **1.24** | 1.30 | 2.83 | 4.34 |
| BY Informatik | **1.92** | 2.22 | 3.45 | 4.31 |
| BY cyber proxy | **1.52** | 1.70 | 3.17 | 3.98 |

Mean MASE over h = 1-5 (h = 1 alone: drift 0.48-0.86). \* conditional check: the subject share held at its last
3-year mean, multiplied by the ACTUAL total (the KMK projection did not exist at past origins).

Reading: drift is the best model in all four series and 2-2.5 times more accurate than naive. Holding the subject
share constant is the worst predictor: the Informatik share of all first-years rose steadily (DE 3.7 % in 2015 ->
5.4 % in 2024), so constant-share projections understate growth if that rise continues. In the other direction, the
backtest period (2012-2024) was a growth phase; drift's errors were mostly under-forecasts, and recent data
(Germany flat since 2023, Bavaria's 2025 dip) argue against simply extrapolating.

## Reading for the argument

1. **The recruiting pool for a cyber bachelor does not shrink.** Even the conservative KMK scenario keeps Bavaria's
   cyber-proxy first-years at about 5,000 in 2030 and 5,500 in 2035 (2024: 4,936), after the 2025 G9 dip.
2. **The range is wide and the direction of risk is known:** constant shares understate (backtests), trend
   extrapolation overstates if the post-2023 flattening holds. Central planning values: Bavaria cyber proxy about
   5,500 (2030) and 6,200 (2035).
3. Germany grows faster than Bavaria in the KMK scenario because Bavaria's school-leaver pool recovers only slowly
   after G9 (Step 42).

## Caveats

- The proxy is not cyber demand; cyber's own share inside it is not observable in Destatis.
- 21 annual points per series; backtests use 12 origins (2012-2023) and overlapping windows (errors are not independent).
- KMK projects state-run institutions and the study year (summer + winter); it is used only as a growth index.
- Winter semesters only (summer starts missing), as in all Destatis-based steps.
