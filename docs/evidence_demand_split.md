## Data

Destatis 21311-0012 (Germany) and 21311-0015 (Bavaria): first-years in their 1st university semester, winter
semesters, German and foreign nationality. Destatis 21321-0003 / -0006: passed exams (graduates). LfStat "Studierende
an den Hochschulen in Bayern" WS 2024/25, Tabelle 3.2: study area Informatik by university type and nationality.

- "Foreign" means foreign nationality. It includes foreigners who went to school in Germany. No table we hold splits
  by where the school-leaving certificate was gained at subject level.
- First-years in their 1st university semester include international master students starting their first semester
  in Germany. Degree level is not available at subject level.
- 2025 is the Bavarian G9 gap year for German school leavers, so 2025 shares overstate the trend.

## What it means for Claim A

- **Absolute growth.** "+6.1 % a year in Bavaria" holds for all first-years. For German first-years, who are the main
  target group of a German-language bachelor, it is +2.3 % in Bavaria and -0.2 % in Germany.
- **Relative position.** Informatik is still the strongest large EMI subject among German first-years. In Bavaria it is
  the only large one that grew (2019-2024): Informatik +2.3 %, Elektrotechnik/Elektronik -5.0 %,
  Wirtschaftsinformatik -3.1 %, Mechatronik -3.5 % a year.
- **Forecast.** The central planning value for 2030 continues a trend that is mostly international. For the German
  target group the careful (KMK) path is the more relevant one.
- **Capture.** The intake estimate (21 / 39 / 75) is not affected. It is anchored on competitors' intakes, which
  already contain their share of foreign students.

## Sensitivity: demand trend scores from German entrants only (all three options)

The same trend anchor (configs/scores.yaml) was applied to German first-years or graduates only, for all three
options; range widths kept, clipped at the scale floor of 1. These scores were proposed by Claude and are for review by
the team.

| Option | Series behind the trend score | German only | Trend score: base -> German only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cybersecurity B.Sc. | cyber proxy first-years | Bavaria +1.9 %, Germany -0.5 % a year | 4 -> 3 (rose weakly, in Bavaria only) |
| Cybersecurity M.Sc. | Informatik graduates | Bavaria +7.2 %, Germany +5.5 % a year | 4 -> 4 (still rising) |
| Robotics + AI B.Sc. | Mechatronik + Technische Informatik first-years | Bavaria -3.3 %, Germany -4.7 % a year | 2 -> 1 (fell over five years) |

Result (10,000 draws, everything else as in the base case):

| Option | P(rank 1), base | P(rank 1), German-only trend |
|---|---|---|
| Cybersecurity B.Sc. | 91.7 % | 78.2 % |
| Cybersecurity M.Sc. | 6.0 % | 19.5 % |
| Robotics + AI B.Sc. | 2.3 % | 2.4 % |

- The cybersecurity bachelor stays first, but at 78.2 % it falls just below the 80 % bar used for "robust".
- Leave-one-criterion-out on top of this sensitivity: the bachelor stays first in five of six runs. Without labour
  relevance, the master is narrowly ahead (50.1 % against 48.2 %).
- Reading: the bachelor's lead is smaller once demand is measured on German entrants, and the master gains, because
  its pipeline (graduates) grows among Germans too. The recommendation does not change, but it is less robust than
  the base case suggests.

## An English-taught programme (option and limitation)

- **For:**
  - Foreign first-years in Informatik grow by about 11 % a year and are now close to half of all first-years.
  - OTH's ministry contract credits new English-taught bachelors with a clear rise in international students
    (docs/evidence_ministry_contracts.md).
  - TH Deggendorf runs English-taught programmes in all faculties.
- **Against:**
  - The only cyber data point is negative. At Saarland University, the English-taught Cybersecurity B.Sc. had 3
    first-semester students in WS 2025/26 (4 in WS 2024/25), against 42 (44) in the German-taught Cybersicherheit
    B.Sc. (docs/evidence_capture.md, plausibility check).
  - Much of the foreign growth is probably master entrants; degree level is not measurable at subject level.
  - The capture model covers Bavarian school leavers only, so it gives no intake estimate for international applicants.
  - The regional employers' need for German-speaking graduates is not measured.
- **Reading:**
  - An English-taught cyber bachelor is not supported as the main variant.
  - International demand fits better with the master. This links to proposal (a): an English-taught cyber master
    later, once the bachelor runs.
  - For the bachelor: English electives at most.

## Caveats

- Nationality is not the same as origin: foreigners with a German school-leaving certificate are counted as foreign.
- The German-only trend scores are a judgement applied with the existing anchor, not a new method; the proxy itself
  is unchanged (decision D3).
