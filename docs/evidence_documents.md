# Evidence from public documents

Status: v1, 14 Oct 2026. Source files: `data/raw/documents/` (local only), checksums in `docs/download_manifest.json`.
Figures marked "own calculation" are derived by the team from the cited tables and must be labelled as such in the report.

## Summary for the decision

1. **Demographic tailwind after 2026, stronger for master than bachelor in Bavaria.** Entrants dip in 2025/26 (Bavaria's return to G9) and recover from 2027. Bavaria's consecutive-master student numbers are forecast to grow while Germany's fall.
2. **Inside engineering, only computer science has grown strongly at Bavarian HAW/TH.** Informatik entrants rose 157 % (2005 to 2023); electrical engineering fell 17 % and mechanical engineering 19 %. Bavarian HAW report rising numbers in new programmes such as AI and renewable energy.
3. **Labour market: shortages sit at the specialist (bachelor-type) level, not the expert level.** In 2025, Informatik, Elektrotechnik and technical R&D are shortage occupations at specialist level; software development at expert level is no longer one.
4. **Programme market is crowded but master supply is large.** 22,426 programmes in WS 2025/26; HAW offer 4,434 bachelor and 3,070 master programmes. 18.2 % of master programmes are English-taught, against 3.7 % of bachelor programmes.

Implications for the protocol: the first cohort of any new programme starts no earlier than WS 2027/28, so it misses the G9 dip. The EMI candidates on the electrical side need a reason to reverse a falling entrant trend; candidates on the computing side face a growing market but more competitors.

## 3. IHF Bayern: entrant trends in engineering at Bavarian HAW/TH

Source: Falk & Lenz, IHF workshop slides, 4 Jul 2025 (`ihf_bayern_ingenieurwiss_2025`). Data: Destatis via ICEland; 1. Hochschulsemester, first degree; computer science counted under engineering since 2015, back-corrected.

Change in first-year students at Bavarian HAW/TH, 2005 to 2023:

| Study area | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Ingenieurwesen allgemein | +391.1 % |
| Informatik | +157.2 % |
| Bauingenieurwesen | +113.7 % |
| Verkehrstechnik, Nautik | +112.5 % |
| Architektur, Innenarchitektur | +35.5 % |
| Wirtschaftsingenieurwesen (engineering focus) | +8.0 % |
| Elektrotechnik und Informationstechnik | −16.8 % |
| Maschinenbau, Verfahrenstechnik | −18.9 % |

Other findings on the slides: first-year students in Bavaria rose 34.5 % from 2005 to 2023 but have stagnated since 2012; engineering entrants in Bavaria fell 1.5 % from 2012 to 2023; foreign engineering entrants at HAW/TH rose 373 % against 40 % for German entrants. In a ministry survey, Bavarian HAW/TH reported rising numbers in new engineering programmes (for example AI, renewable energy) and in interdisciplinary programmes, and named internationalisation as a driver.

Caveat: percentages from a small base (Ingenieurwesen allgemein) overstate size. Use GENESIS counts for the real levels.

## 4. HRK: programme market, WS 2025/26

Source: HRK, *Statistische Daten zu Studienangeboten an Hochschulen in Deutschland, WS 2025/2026*, Oct 2025 (`hrk_statistik_ws_2025_26`). Based on Hochschulkompass and Destatis.

- 22,426 programmes at 422 institutions: 10,218 bachelor, 10,492 master.
- HAW/FH: 4,434 bachelor and 3,070 master programmes. Universities: 5,045 bachelor, 6,795 master.
- Engineering: 4,159 programmes (2,232 bachelor, 1,868 master). Bavaria offers 3,139 programmes in total.
- English-taught: 2,383 programmes (10.6 %); 3.7 % of first-degree and 18.2 % of further-degree programmes.
- 29.8 % of first-degree programmes have restricted admission; 66.6 % of master programmes are open admission.
- First-year students, study year 2024: 490,304 (+1.7 %). Students WS 2024/25: 2,864,122, of which engineering 748,619 (26.1 %). Graduates 2024: 483,435, of which engineering 124,629.

Caveat stated by the HRK: Hochschulkompass subject groups are not comparable with Destatis subject statistics. Reuse terms: reprinting or use in electronic systems needs HRK's written permission, so cite figures and do not redistribute the PDF.

## 5. BA: shortage analysis 2025

Source: Statistik der Bundesagentur für Arbeit, *Fachkräfteengpassanalyse 2025*, Mai 2026 (`ba_engpassanalyse_2025`; copy via frauundberuf-bw.de, title page checked).

- 157 shortage occupations in 2025 (2024: 163; 2023: 183). Over half at skilled-worker level, about a third at specialist level (48), a sixth at expert level (25).
- Method: six equally weighted indicators (vacancy time, jobseeker-to-vacancy ratio, occupation unemployment rate, change in foreign employment share, exit rate from unemployment, wage growth); an occupation with average score from 2.0 to 3.0 is a shortage occupation.
- **Specialist level** shortage occupations with high employment include 4310 Informatik (score 2.0, newly added in 2025), 2630 Elektrotechnik (2.2), 2710 technische Forschung und Entwicklung (2.0) and 2513 technical maintenance services (2.0).
- **Expert level**: software development is no longer a shortage occupation in 2025; neither is IT sales at specialist level.
- The BA states there is no general labour shortage; the analysis is not a forecast.

Mapping caveat: in the German classification of occupations (KldB 2010), "Spezialist" roughly covers master craftsperson, technician or bachelor-level work, and "Experte" covers work usually requiring at least four years of university study. Treat this as an approximate mapping to bachelor and master graduates, and state it in the report.

Usage terms (from the report): data and tables may be used freely with source citation; own calculations allowed if labelled; online publication should link to the BA statistics homepage.
