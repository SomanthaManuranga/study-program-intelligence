# 9. How sure we are, and the limits

<!-- Plain English. Numbers only as {{key}} placeholders. Ordered by how much each limit could change the answer. -->

We are fairly sure about the ranking and much less sure about the exact number of students. The limits below are
ordered by how much they could change our answer.

1. **No real first-year numbers.** None of the similar programmes we checked publishes how many students start. Our
   student estimate rests on one planned capacity and one student review. In the low case, only
   {{cyber_bsc_netnew_low}} of the {{cyber_bsc_gross_low}} students a year would be new to OTH. That is below our
   minimum of {{min_cohort}}.
2. **Cybersecurity is measured indirectly.** No official statistic counts cybersecurity students. We read demand from
   computer science, from online searches and from job adverts.
3. **The capture model is simple.** It uses straight-line distances and only students from Bavaria. Students from
   Saxony, Thuringia and further away are missing, so this part makes our estimate too low rather than too high. The
   model may also understate how many students move between two very similar programmes.
4. **Uneven evidence.** For robotics we could not obtain a verified student number for a comparable programme. We assumed a
   wide range of first-year numbers for such a programme and gave its scores wider ranges.
5. **Some scoring details came late.** The weights and kill rules came first. The scoring scales for five of the six
   points, and part of the sixth, were written after we had collected the evidence, but before the first ranking.
6. **Module comparison.** The computer program compares descriptions, not real teaching. It tends to count too much as
   already taught.
7. **Staff and cost.** The staff need is an estimate, and we did not estimate the cost of the lab. We did not read the
   research profiles of OTH staff.
8. **Job adverts are signals, not jobs.** One advert can mention several keywords, and the job board does not list
   every advert.
9. **Missing sources.** Some databases and pages blocked automatic downloads or needed a login, or our login did not work. We
   did not work around that. One school year is missing from the school-leaver data.

**What we tested.** We left out each scoring point in turn. We assumed that robotics takes half its students from
OTH. We let the student numbers of the options vary independently, and we gave robotics more favourable ranges. The
winner did not change. Its lowest share of first places was {{loo_min_cyber_bsc}}. Not every limit was tested this way. For example,
we did not rerun the ranking with half of the cybersecurity students coming from OTH.

**Bottom line.** The limits mainly affect how big the programme would be, not which option comes first. That is why our
first recommendation is to get one real first-year number before deciding.
