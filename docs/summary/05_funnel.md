# 5. How we narrowed down the options

<!-- Plain English. Numbers only as {{key}} placeholders. -->

Imagine a hiring panel that writes down its rules before it reads any application. Then nobody can say the panel picked
its favourite first and invented the rules afterwards. We did the same for the weights and the kill rules. On {{rules_fixed_date}} we first fixed the six scoring
points with their weights, then the five kill rules. The project history shows that the kill rules came about five
hours before we screened the first data on study fields.

Then we narrowed the options down step by step:

1. **Start:** {{funnel_0_themes}} related to the faculty, such as artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, robotics,
   energy and media.
2. **Do students still want the field?** We dropped media technology and games. In media technology, first-year numbers
   in Germany changed by {{funnel_medientechnik}} a year. We kept robotics, although its related subjects are
   flat or falling, because it could be combined with artificial intelligence. {{funnel_1_demand}} fields were left.
3. **Is there a gap near OTH?** We dropped general artificial intelligence, because there are already
   {{funnel_ai_ba_150km}} bachelor and {{funnel_ai_ma_150km}} master offers within 150 km and OTH offers both. We also dropped
   themes that OTH already offers, and put microelectronics on a watch list because very few students start it. This
   left {{funnel_2_candidates}}.
4. **Does OTH teach most of it already?** Kill rule 1 removed a master in embedded systems, because OTH already teaches
   {{funnel_embedded_overlap}} of its core topics, and a part-time AI master, because OTH already has one. This left
   {{funnel_3_shortlist}} options.
5. **Other kill rules:** none of the three failed the rules on staff, falling demand or a falling supply of graduates.
6. **Ranking:** the comparison of the three options in Section 8.

{{fig:f00_funnel}}

The funnel is not perfect, and we say where.

- The two screening filters (falling demand, crowded supply) were set on the day of the screening itself.
- The fields that OTH already offers were first dropped without a written reason. A later check gave all
  {{themes_all}} themes a written verdict with the same filters. On the same filters no theme came out stronger than
  cybersecurity; the next step was judged on titles only.
- Robotics stayed in although {{robotics_overlap}} of its core topics are already taught in one OTH programme, the AI
  bachelor. The kill rule only drops options above half.
- The scoring scales for five of the six points, and part of the sixth, were written after we had collected the
  evidence, but before the first ranking.
- Weak evidence widens a score's range instead of raising it: this rule came before the first ranking; that the
  widening must be even came after it.
- The bar for calling the result robust was set when we reported the results (Section 8).
