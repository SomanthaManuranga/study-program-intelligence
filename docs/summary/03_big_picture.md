# 3. The big picture: four questions

<!-- Plain English. Numbers only as {{key}} placeholders. -->

Think of a company that wants to open a new shop. It would ask four things before it signs a lease. Do people want the
product? Would they come to this shop, or go to a rival? Can the company make the product with its staff? And is there a
future for the business? We asked the same four questions about a new programme:

1. **Do students want it?** We looked at how many people start studying related subjects each year, and how this may
   change until 2035.
2. **Would they come to OTH?** We looked at how many school leavers finish school near OTH, where the nearest similar
   programmes are, how far students usually travel, and how many students similar programmes plan for.
3. **Can OTH teach it?** We compared the course plans of similar programmes with what OTH's faculty already teaches. We
   estimated the extra staff and equipment, but not the cost of the lab.
4. **Are there jobs?** We counted job adverts and looked at employment statistics.

We answered these questions in full for the cybersecurity bachelor and with less evidence for the other two finalists.
Earlier candidates were screened on student demand and competition, and two of them also on the overlap rule.

Two more checks came after the four questions. First, we asked whether a new programme would only take students away from
OTH's existing programmes. We call this **cannibalisation**. It is like a new café that mainly takes customers from the
owner's other café down the street. Second, we compared the options in **what-if scenarios**. We repeated the comparison
{{mc_draws}} times. Each time, the weights of the six scoring points changed a little, and each score was drawn from its
range, from low to high. Then we counted how often each option came first.

Before the first screening, we fixed how much each of the six scoring points would count. We also fixed five **kill
rules**. A kill rule drops an option at once. One example: more than half of its core topics are already taught in one
existing OTH programme at the same level. Fixing these rules early makes it harder for our own preferences to shape the
result. Some rules came later, and Section 5 says which.
