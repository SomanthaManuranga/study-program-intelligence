# Recommendation and next steps

## Recommendation

**EMI should introduce a Cybersecurity B.Sc.** It is the only shortlisted option for which all four claims hold:
demand in its field grows (Section 3), OTH has a regional pool without a full-time dedicated competitor nearby
(Section 4), EMI can carry the foundations and needs a costed security core (Section 5), and graduates meet a large,
Bavarian-heavy job market with a shortage at bachelor level (Section 6). Most of its students would be new to OTH
(Section 7), and it ranks first in {{p_rank1_cyber_bsc_base}} of the Monte Carlo draws (Section 8).

Conditions for the recommendation:

- **Size:** plan for a central intake of about {{cyber_bsc_gross_central}} first-years a year at steady state, with a
  range of {{cyber_bsc_gross_low}} to {{cyber_bsc_gross_high}}; plan the first years below steady state.
- **Resources:** {{new_professorships}} (offensive security and forensics; defensive and OT security), a lab engineer,
  teaching assignments for law and security management, and an isolated security lab, ideally linked to the existing
  automation labs.
- **Profile:** a broad security bachelor with an industrial (OT) module and electives, using the English term
  "Cyber Security" in the name, which is how prospective students search (Section 3). OT is a useful accent, not a
  differentiator against TH Deggendorf.
- **Timing:** the first intake after accreditation, and not in a G9 gap year.

## Before the decision: narrow the intake range

The cheapest way to reduce the main uncertainty is one measured number: the actual first-year intakes of TH
Ingolstadt's and TH Deggendorf's cyber bachelors, obtained through the universities or the Bavarian State Office for
Statistics. If they are near TH Ingolstadt's planned capacity, OTH should plan conservatively and check the low case
against its own minimum cohort; if they are near the Deggendorf figure, the central case is conservative.

## Proposals (team judgement, not model results)

**(a) The Cyber/OT M.Sc. as a follow-on.** The master ranks second. We propose to offer it later, once the bachelor
produces its own graduates: it would then build on the bachelor's staff and lab, recruit the bachelor's graduates and
reduce cannibalisation of AI for Industrial Applications and IT und Automation. The second new professorship could
carry its OT specialisation.

**(b) Robotics as a specialisation within the KI B.Sc.** Robotics + AI ranks last. {{robotics_overlap}} of its core
topics are already taught in OTH's KI B.Sc., and if half of its students came from OTH programmes, only
{{robotics_netnew_cannib50}} a year would be new to OTH. Strengthening the existing Smart Robotics specialisation of
the KI B.Sc. would use the strong local robotics job market without a new programme competing with an existing one.
